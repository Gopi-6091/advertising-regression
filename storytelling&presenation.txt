Phase 10 — Business Storytelling & Presentation

The goal is not to present every notebook cell.

A hiring manager should understand your project in roughly this sequence:

Business problem → Data → Evidence → Model → Validation → Finding → Business implication → Limitation

1. The 30-second project story

If someone asks:

“Tell me about your Advertising Regression project.”

You should eventually be able to say:

I analyzed advertising expenditure across TV, Radio, and Newspaper to understand their relationship with product sales and build a model for predicting sales. I first audited and explored the data, then developed simple and multiple linear regression models and evaluated them using train/test metrics and cross-validation. TV and Radio provided strong predictive and statistical evidence, while Newspaper added little additional value. The final TV + Radio model achieved a cross-validated RMSE of about 1.66 sales units, but because the data is observational, I treat the results as predictive associations rather than causal effects or a direct solution for optimal budget allocation.

That's your project elevator pitch.

2. The 5–7 minute presentation structure

I recommend approximately 8 slides.

Slide 1 — Business Problem

Title:

Advertising Spend and Sales: Regression Analysis

Explain:

Company invests in TV, Radio, Newspaper.
Business wants to understand how advertising relates to Sales.
Analytical goal: predict Sales.
Business goal: provide evidence that may support advertising decisions.

Important: Immediately state:

This is observational data, so the analysis estimates associations rather than causal effects.

Slide 2 — Data Understanding & Quality

Show:

200 observations
TV
Radio
Newspaper
Sales
Unnamed: 0 identified as an index/identifier

Then briefly mention:

No missing values
No duplicate rows
No obvious data-entry errors
Potential Newspaper outliers investigated and retained

Don't spend two minutes on cleaning.

The message is:

“I verified that the data was suitable for analysis before modeling.”

3. EDA — What did we discover?

This should be a visual slide.

Show:

Sales vs TV
Sales vs Radio
Sales vs Newspaper

Your story:

TV showed the strongest visual relationship with Sales, Radio showed a moderate positive relationship, and Newspaper showed a substantially weaker relationship.

Then show the correlation values:

Predictor	Sales correlation
TV	0.782
Radio	0.576
Newspaper	0.228

And immediately add:

Correlation describes linear association, not causation.

4. Modeling Strategy

Don't simply say:

"I ran linear regression."

Show the progression.

Model 1

TV → Sales

Purpose:

Understand one predictor before introducing multiple predictors.

Then:

Model 2

TV + Radio + Newspaper → Sales

Purpose:

Evaluate the combined predictive relationship.

Then:

Model selection

Cross-validation compared:

TV + Radio
TV + Radio + Newspaper

Result:

Model	CV RMSE
TV + Radio	1.663
TV + Radio + Newspaper	1.682

Therefore:

TV + Radio was preferred because it performed slightly better while remaining simpler.

This is a very good modeling story because you aren't saying:

"Newspaper had a high p-value, therefore I deleted it."

You're showing that statistical evidence + predictive performance + simplicity informed the decision.

5. Statistical Evidence

This is where you demonstrate that you're more than someone who simply knows LinearRegression().

Show the multiple regression coefficients:

Predictor	Coefficient	p-value
TV	0.0447	<0.001
Radio	0.1892	<0.001
Newspaper	0.0028	0.696

Explain:

TV has statistically significant positive association.
Radio has statistically significant positive association.
Newspaper does not provide statistically significant evidence of an independent linear association after accounting for TV and Radio.

Then say:

Statistical significance and predictive usefulness are related but different questions.

That's an excellent interview signal.

6. Model Validation & Diagnostics

This slide answers:

“How do you know your model isn't just giving you a nice-looking equation?”

Show:

Predictive validation
Train/test evaluation
Cross-validation
RMSE
MAE
R²

Current preferred model:

CV RMSE ≈ 1.663 sales units

Diagnostics

You investigated:

residual distribution
residuals vs fitted
Q-Q plot
Breusch-Pagan
VIF
Cook's distance

Overall:

No major regression assumption violations were identified, although some tail deviations, possible mild curvature, and influential observations remain limitations.

This demonstrates model skepticism, which is valuable.

7. Business Findings

Now stop talking about statistics.

Talk like a data scientist advising the business.

Finding 1

TV and Radio are useful predictors of Sales.

Finding 2

Newspaper provides limited additional evidence once TV and Radio are included.

Finding 3

TV + Radio provides slightly better cross-validated predictive performance with fewer predictors.

Finding 4

The model can be used for sales prediction and spending scenarios.

But:

It does not establish that increasing advertising spending will causally increase Sales.

8. Recommendation & Limitations

This is arguably the most important slide.

Recommendation

Use the TV + Radio model as a predictive/scenario-analysis tool, not as a standalone budget-optimization system.

For example, the business could ask:

"Given this proposed TV and Radio spending combination, what Sales does the model predict?"

But we should not conclude:

"The optimal budget is X% TV and Y% Radio."

Why?

Because optimization requires additional information:

causal effects
spending constraints
channel costs
diminishing returns
profit margins
business objectives

The hiring-manager version

If I were evaluating this project, the strongest thing isn't:

“I achieved an R² of 0.899.”

It's this:

“I started with a business question, investigated the data, formed hypotheses, built progressively more complex models, evaluated them on unseen data and through cross-validation, checked regression assumptions, and then translated the statistical findings into a business recommendation while explicitly identifying what the model cannot establish.”

That is the data-science story.
